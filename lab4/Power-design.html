Power tree and battery decision

We selected the CNHL S220403BK 3S LiPo (11.1 V nominal, 12.6 V full, 2.2 Ah) and Hitec 44295 balance charger. Anson approved both Amazon listings on October 28. A pack-side fuse protects the vehicle adapter before J40; the PCB fuse and reverse-polarity PMOS feed the protected battery node. This supplies the L298 and the separate 6.6 V servo, 5.1 V Pi/devices and 3.318 V logic converters. The encoders now have a separate 4.0 V linear regulator from 5.1 V. See battery, charging and cutoff design.

The regulator design envelope remains 8–15 V. The low end is not a LiPo discharge target. We propose a 10.5 V loaded drive stop; the new ADC divider/filter supports this, while firmware integration and the physical shutdown demonstration remain unfinished. Keep motor/servo current returns away from analog and Pi return paths. Charge off-board using standard 3S LiPo balance mode in the required bag.

Component choices and calculations

A buck converter switches its input and smooths the pulses with an inductor and capacitors to make a lower voltage. Three independent converters isolate load disturbances better than one shared servo/logic supply. They still share the battery, so battery sag can affect all three.

TPS564252 feedback reference is nominally 0.6 V. Vout = 0.6 × (1 + Rtop/Rbottom). Use 100 kΩ/10 kΩ for 6.6 V, 75 kΩ/10 kΩ for 5.1 V, and 45.3 kΩ/10 kΩ for 3.318 V. The 5.1 V pair uses 0.1% parts: with a ±1.5% feedback reference, the calculated static endpoints are about 5.015–5.186 V before wiring loss, transients and other error. A 1% pair gives approximately 5.268 V at the worst high corner, so it is not our choice for this rail.

Each converter uses a 4.7 µH Eaton SDCHA1V8040-4R7-R inductor. Input banks are now three 22 µF/25 V 1210 capacitors plus 100 nF. Servo/Pi/logic output banks are four/three/three of the same 22 µF part (88/66/66 µF nominal), with 68/91/30 pF C0G feed-forward capacitors. The revised bias, tolerance, temperature and aging sensitivity passes our effective-capacitance targets. See the capacitor-bank review for sources and limits. Startup, load-transient and thermal performance are unmeasured.

At Vin = 15 V and f = 600 kHz, ripple ΔIL = Vout(Vin−Vout)/(Vin L f). Peak inductor current ≈ Iout + ΔIL/2. The following comparison includes −20% inductance tolerance, but not all saturation, temperature and frequency effects.

The Eaton part has 5.8 A thermal and 6.7 A saturation ratings under its datasheet conditions. This check does not establish regulator thermal capability. A chip's 4 A rating depends on PCB copper, cooling and operating conditions.

EN is an enable input, not a battery measurement output. Divider pairs 47 kΩ/10 kΩ, 33 kΩ/10 kΩ and 22 kΩ/10 kΩ keep EN below its recommended 5.5 V at 15 V input. Including the nominal internal 2 MΩ pull-down, typical rising thresholds are 6.81 V, 5.14 V and 3.82 V. These are enabling thresholds, not proof the rails regulate at those voltages. PG is an open-drain power-good signal pulled up to 3.3 V; it is not an independent battery cutoff.

Q1 is DMPH3010LK3-13: drain toward the fused battery, source toward the load; 10 kΩ gate pull-down, 47 kΩ gate-to-source resistor and 12 V gate zener. The zener cathode goes to the source. This protects against reversed battery polarity, but does not block reverse current during braking or hot-plug events. At 10 A and 10 mΩ, Q1 dissipates 1 W at the quoted cold resistance; hot resistance and PCB thermal behavior need checking.

C400 is a proposed 1000 µF/25 V Nichicon UPW1E102MHD, 12.5 mm diameter × 20 mm high, 5 mm lead pitch. The datasheet high-frequency ripple rating is 1.66 A under its specified conditions, not permission for arbitrary motor ripple. Bulk capacitance handles short edges: an extra 4 A for 1 ms would change an ideal 1000 µF capacitor by 4 V. It cannot replace an adequate battery and holder.

The October 25 regulator review checks pin connectivity, resistor/reference corners and enable-pin voltage bounds. We also corrected the switching-pin orientation and tightened local placement. Calculated results do not establish capacitor bias performance, fault saturation margin, routed-loop quality or thermal capability.

Input current and runtime

Use power when combining different rails: Ibat ≈ Imotors + [6.6Iservo/ηservo + 5.1I5V/η5V + 3.318I3V3/η3V3]/Vbat. Do not simply add their output currents. At all three provisional allocations, converted output power is 45.88 W. With an assumed 85% efficiency and the raw 6.4 A motor stress case, battery current would be about 10.90 A at 12 V and 13.15 A at 8 V. This deliberately shows that the handout's 10 A estimate is not a verified maximum. It is not a requested test or safe L298 load.

The selected 11.1 V/2.2 Ah LiPo has 24.42 Wh nominal energy. Runtime ≈ usable Wh / average battery power. We have not established average power or usable capacity under the vehicle load, so this is not a measured runtime prediction.

The existing four-rail planning estimates are complete as estimates. Current-evidence.md now includes 8–15 V and 80%/85% efficiency sensitivity cases. Lower efficiency raises predicted input current; neither assumption certifies a battery rating. New-board voltage, noise and thermal tests are later bring-up work. No repeat measurement campaign is required solely by those guiding questions; follow any specific TA instruction.

Remaining work by stage

  • Before ordering/assembly: confirm stock shortfalls, servo harness method and solder process. The battery/charger pair and PCB geometry are selected.
  • Before LiPo use: qualify the installed main fuse/harness, integrate and test the battery guard, inspect the assembled board and commission from a current-limited bench supply. Record startup, actual car workload, ripple and temperature.
  • Optional/deferred: sustained 3 A Pi operation, extra peripherals or hardware limiting, deliberate stall tests, 2 oz redesign and expanded simulation. These are not prototype fabrication blockers.

Lab 4 power design and calculations

23 October 2026. Calculated design for review; no new circuit has been built or measured. This engineering supplement supports the shared Lab 4 Writeup.

Power tree and battery decision

Ten-cell Sub-C NiMH pack → keyed connector → fuse → reverse-polarity PMOS → protected battery node. That node feeds the L298 motor branch and three independent TPS564252 buck converters: 6.6 V servo, 5.1 V Pi/devices, and 3.318 V logic, including both encoders. Grounds are common; route motor and servo return currents to the input bulk/ground region without carrying them through analog or Pi ground connections. Preserve a continuous signal reference plane.

Battery and charger purchasing remain unresolved under the October 24 DigiKey/Mouser rule. Tenergy 11609/01025 remains a documented design reference with zero purchase quantity; the Dantona candidates lack verified discharge limits and compatible-charger evidence. The BOM explicitly requests one unresolved pack and charger. No on-board charger is included. See Parts-review.md for the sources and exceptions.

The protected-node electrical qualification range is 8–15 V. This is not permission to discharge a ten-cell pack to 8 V. Set the operating stop voltage from the selected cell data and weakest-cell behavior, with margin for servo regulation; do not rely on converter undervoltage lockout to protect NiMH cells. Maximum charged voltage and switching transients must stay within component ratings.

Component choices and calculations

A buck converter switches its input and smooths the pulses with an inductor and capacitors to make a lower voltage. Three independent converters isolate load disturbances better than one shared servo/logic supply. They still share the battery, so battery sag can affect all three.

TPS564252 feedback reference is nominally 0.6 V. Vout = 0.6 × (1 + Rtop/Rbottom). Use 100 kΩ/10 kΩ for 6.6 V, 75 kΩ/10 kΩ for 5.1 V, and 45.3 kΩ/10 kΩ for 3.318 V. The 5.1 V pair uses 0.1% parts: with a ±1.5% feedback reference, the calculated static endpoints are about 5.015–5.186 V before wiring loss, transients and other error. A 1% pair gives approximately 5.268 V at the worst high corner, so it is not our choice for this rail.

Each converter has a proposed 4.7 µH Eaton SDCHA1V8040-4R7-R inductor, 22 µF/25 V plus 100 nF/50 V input bypass, two 22 µF/16 V output capacitors, and 22 pF feed-forward capacitance. TI's 5 V example uses 4.7 µH; its 3.3 V example uses 3.3 µH. Our common 4.7 µH choice and the 6.6 V circuit still need loop/transient validation. The two output capacitors total 44 µF only at nominal test conditions; DC bias reduces ceramic capacitance.

At Vin = 15 V and f = 600 kHz, ripple ΔIL = Vout(Vin−Vout)/(Vin L f). Peak inductor current ≈ Iout + ΔIL/2. The following comparison includes −20% inductance tolerance, but not all saturation, temperature and frequency effects.

The Eaton part has 5.8 A thermal and 6.7 A saturation ratings under its datasheet conditions. This check does not establish regulator thermal capability. A chip's 4 A rating depends on PCB copper, cooling and operating conditions.

EN is an enable input, not a battery measurement output. Divider pairs 47 kΩ/10 kΩ, 33 kΩ/10 kΩ and 22 kΩ/10 kΩ keep EN below its recommended 5.5 V at 15 V input. Including the nominal internal 2 MΩ pull-down, typical rising thresholds are 6.81 V, 5.14 V and 3.82 V. These are enabling thresholds, not proof the rails regulate at those voltages. PG is an open-drain power-good signal pulled up to 3.3 V; it is not an independent battery cutoff.

Q1 is DMPH3010LK3-13: drain toward the fused battery, source toward the load; 10 kΩ gate pull-down, 47 kΩ gate-to-source resistor and 12 V gate zener. The zener cathode goes to the source. This protects against reversed battery polarity, but does not block reverse current during braking or hot-plug events. At 10 A and 10 mΩ, Q1 dissipates 1 W at the quoted cold resistance; hot resistance and PCB thermal behavior need checking.

C400 is a proposed 1000 µF/25 V Nichicon UPW1E102MHD, 12.5 mm diameter × 20 mm high, 5 mm lead pitch. The datasheet high-frequency ripple rating is 1.66 A under its specified conditions, not permission for arbitrary motor ripple. Bulk capacitance handles short edges: an extra 4 A for 1 ms would change an ideal 1000 µF capacitor by 4 V. It cannot replace an adequate battery and holder.

Input current and runtime

Use power when combining different rails: Ibat ≈ Imotors + [6.6Iservo/ηservo + 5.1I5V/η5V + 3.318I3V3/η3V3]/Vbat. Do not simply add their output currents. At all three provisional allocations, converted output power is 45.88 W. With an assumed 85% efficiency and the raw 6.4 A motor stress case, battery current would be about 10.90 A at 12 V and 13.15 A at 8 V. This deliberately shows that the handout's 10 A estimate is not a verified maximum. It is not a requested test or safe L298 load.

The selected pack has nominal energy 12 V × 4.2 Ah = 50.4 Wh. Runtime ≈ usable Wh / average battery power. We have not established average power or usable capacity under the vehicle load, so this is not a measured runtime prediction.

Open decisions before manufacture

  • Resolve a permitted battery/charger pair, operating cutoff and mounting; runtime is unmeasured.
  • Fuse element coordinated with holder/wire/connector limits and measured startup. J40 now uses a Hirose DF63M-2P-3.96DSA power header. Verify mating contacts, wire gauge, harness polarity and derating.
  • Motor current limiting, braking behavior and L298 heatsink; input transient suppression or clamp if measurements require it.
  • Effective capacitor values, buck loop stability, load-step sag/overshoot and heat; PCB current paths and copper widths.
  • Both MCU support circuits and cross-sheet power connections are now placed. The 284-component schematic passes ERC. Actuator interfaces and an unrouted placement are included, but motor current limiting, heatsink selection, detailed routing and hardware validation remain unfinished. No PCB has been released.
